A simple guide to tyre pressure gauges

Checking tyre pressures with a suitable gauge is a useful routine, especially before road trips or carrying heavy loads.

Quick checklist

  • Read the tyre placard
  • Check pressures when cold
  • Include the spare if fitted

Find your vehicle specification

Use the tyre placard or vehicle handbook for the pressure specified for your vehicle, tyre size and load. Do not confuse the maximum figure on the tyre sidewall with the vehicle maker’s recommended setting.

Digital or analogue?

A digital gauge can be easy to read, particularly in dim light. A mechanical dial gauge avoids batteries. Whatever the style, choose a gauge with a scale that covers the pressures you need and a head that seals well against the valve.

Check tyres cold

Where possible check before driving, when the tyres are cold. Remove the valve cap, hold the gauge firmly on the valve and note the measurement. Adjust to the vehicle maker’s recommendation and replace the cap.

Remember the spare

If your car has a conventional spare tyre, check it as well. A pressure gauge will not tell you whether a tyre is damaged or worn, so include a visual inspection.
